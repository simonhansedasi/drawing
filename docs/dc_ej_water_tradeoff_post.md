Day 12 of Visualizing the Unseen [until I get hired]: The desert campus isn't the burden hotspot

Washington's data-center boom gets covered as a water story: Quincy's high-desert campuses draw headlines for how much water they pull in a place that barely gets any. So I plotted merascope's own scored data against a different axis: environmental-justice burden. Every confirmed, active WA data-center site, matched to its real ZIP-code-area (ZCTA) score for water availability and EJ burden.

The two don't track together -- they run backwards. Tukwila's cluster has the best water access of any site in the state, and the worst EJ score. Quincy's cluster -- the driest water score in the state -- reads as *less* of an EJ burden. The place getting the water headlines isn't the place carrying the community-burden weight.

Not a claim that Quincy's water draw is fine -- that's real and it's a separate question. This is just what happens when you look at community burden instead of water, on the same map, using the same pipeline.
