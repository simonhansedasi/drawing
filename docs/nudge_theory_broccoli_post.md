Week 46, and it only took almost a year of dada science before I learned about Nudge Theory.

If you have children (or employees), listen up.

Nudge theory, in short: people don't respond to incentives the way you'd expect them to. An incentive doesn't just pull someone toward the reward, it turns everything standing between them and that reward into a hurdle, something to get through rather than something to actually engage with. Take the incentive away and the hurdle disappears with it, so the thing you actually wanted them to do stops being an obstacle and just becomes the thing in front of them.

For instance: my toddler will eat broccoli and he'll eat chicken nuggets. If the nuggies and broccoli show up at the same time, the greens typically are eaten last if at all. However, if I serve broccoli while the chicken is cooking, the broccoli is no longer a blocker for the breaded-dino-shaped-friends and just becomes a yummy treat instead.

He's not even three, but this isn't a toddler thing. Gate a report behind a bonus conversation and the report gets whatever clears the gate, not your best work. Review the same report on its own, bonus discussed some other time, and it gets done properly, because it's just the job now, not a toll booth.
