Visualizing the Unseen (until I get hired): Back pain from vacuuming carpet? You might be too tall.

Push a vacuum the natural way -- along the rod, since anything else twists your wrist -- and that push splits into forward motion and straight-down force, in a ratio set by the angle between the rod and the floor.

That angle comes from your elbow height, which comes from your own height. Too tall, and the angle tips too steep: your push drives down into the carpet instead of forward, and the vacuum barely moves.

The instinctive fix is to hunch at the waist -- it also happens to get you closer to the carpet for inspection. But that just moves the load onto your lower back.

Better: widen your stance into something closer to a lunge. That drops your elbow height without bending your spine, and lets you rock forward and back with each stroke instead of hunching over it.

An oversight in vacuum design, turned into an accidental workout.

Visualizng the unseen (until I get hired): Back pain from vacuuming carpet? You might be too tall.

Push a vacuum the natural way and the push splits into forward motion and straight-down force, in a ratio set by the angle between the rod and the floor.

This angle is also determined by the elbow height of the vacuum cleaner operator. Too high of an angle, and the push from the operator is driven into the ground, not moving forward.

In order to reduce the angle, the operator must shorten themselves to get an effective push angle and overcome friction. It is an easy first instict to maintain a stance and hunch at the waist. This appears to also get the visual closer to the carpet for inspection. There is, however, a better way.

Widen your stance as if entering a lunge. This will prevent your lower back from carrying the weight. You may also rock back and forth as you strike with the vacuum, creating a lunge work-out while cleaning.

An over-sight of vacuum cleaning design, turned into an accidental workout.
