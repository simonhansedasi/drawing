Day 2 of visualizing the unseen. What is hiding in plain sight until you layer the proper data? Where should transit expansion be prioritized?

Snohomish County, WA has 575 census block groups. Split them into two maps: who actually needs transit (elderly, low-income, no-vehicle, unemployed households) and how much service actually reaches them (frequency-weighted by how often the bus really comes, not just whether a stop exists nearby).

Neither map alone tells you where the problem is. Need is spread across the rural east. Access is concentrated almost entirely in a narrow I-5 corridor; about 70% of block groups score under 5% accessibility.

Overlay them and the gap is unmistakable: the same rural and southern block groups a live transit-equity model independently flags as highest priority — Everett, Stanwood, Darrington.

Maps and analysis for Snohomish and King Counties, Washington are hosted at snotrac.simonhansedasi.com.
