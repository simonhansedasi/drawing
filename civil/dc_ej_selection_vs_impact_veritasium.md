If a group of places looks poorer on average than similar places, something about those specific places is causing it. Right?

Pull the Census numbers and it looks confirmed: the ~1,200 ZIP codes hosting a confirmed data center are poorer than the rest, and have a meaningfully higher minority population share. Either data centers get built where communities were already struggling, or they make things worse once they land. Pick a side and you've got a story.

Before picking a side I split the same numbers one more way: by minority status. Look only at the white residents of these ZIP codes: hosting and non-hosting are at parity on poverty. Look only at the minority residents: same thing, parity. If anything, hosting comes out slightly better. Neither population shows a real gap on its own. Put both populations back together inside each ZIP code and a gap reappears, out of nowhere.

That's Simpson's Paradox, and the most famous case of it is a real lawsuit. UC Berkeley's 1973 graduate admissions looked damning in aggregate: 44% of male applicants got in, only 35% of women, a gap too large to be chance. Statisticians dug into the actual departments and found the opposite story hiding inside: only 4 of 85 departments were significantly biased against women, 6 were significantly biased against men, and the majority showed no real gap either way. The overall number wasn't lying exactly, but it also wasn't measuring what everyone assumed. Women were applying more to the departments that reject nearly everyone, men included; blend a low-admit department and a high-admit department together and the blend just reflects who applied where.

Same mechanism, different data. Hosting ZIP codes have a different racial mix than non-hosting ones, 10 to 13 points more minority residents, and minority poverty runs structurally higher than white poverty everywhere in the country, with or without a data center anywhere nearby. Blend two groups that individually show almost no gap, but at a different ratio, and the blend inherits whichever group's rate the mix leans toward.

Two things could cause that pooled gap: composition, a different mix alone moving the average with nobody worse off; rate, same-race residents actually faring worse in hosting ZIPs. Only rate implicates a data center. Split by race and composition does nearly all the work; rate stays near zero.

The uncomfortable part: a real effect and a composition artifact produce the exact same pooled chart. The only way to tell them apart is to open the aggregate up and check what's inside it, and most numbers people cite never take that step.
